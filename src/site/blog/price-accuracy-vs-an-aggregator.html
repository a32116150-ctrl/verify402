We publish how far our prices are from an aggregator's, every day

28 trades, 7 networks, one independent reference, and every gap on a public page, including the ones that make us look bad.

verify402's paid route check answers one question: what would the chain actually pay for this swap right now? It reads the pools directly at a pinned block and returns the best venue it found. An agent uses that number to decide whether a quote it was offered is fair. If our number is off, the agent's decision is off, so we should be able to show how good it is.

Saying "accurate" is cheap. So there is now a page that measures it: vex402.online/accuracy.

How it works

Once a day, a scan quotes the same trades two ways at the same moment: through verify402's route check, and through KyberSwap's public route API, an independent DEX aggregator. The trades are a dollar stablecoin to the network's main coin and back, at $1,000 and $25,000, on Ethereum, Base, Arbitrum, OP Mainnet, BNB Chain, Polygon and Avalanche. That is 28 trades a day.

For each trade the page shows our best venue, both amounts, and the gap: (ours / aggregator − 1), as a percentage. Positive means our quote pays more. Negative means the aggregator found more. The raw data is at /accuracy.json, and a 90-day history builds up as the scan runs.

What the first run showed

All 28 trades were measured. All 14 trades at $1,000 were within 0.5% of the aggregator, most within a few hundredths of a percent. The typical gap across all trades was about 0.13%.

Large trades were the honest weak spot. On Polygon, a $25,000 trade was about 4.5% behind the aggregator, and BNB Chain was about 0.5% behind. The cause was simple: for a trade like USDC to WPOL we only looked at direct pools, while the aggregator also went through USDT first, where the book is deeper. We added that path. In our own test after the change, the Polygon gap fell to about 0.24% and BNB Chain to about 0.08%. The daily page will show whether that holds.

What it does not show

An aggregator can split one order across many pools and sources we do not read, including private market makers that quote off-chain. On large trades it is expected to pay somewhat more than a quote read from public pools, and we show that gap rather than hide it. The aggregator's number is its own route estimate, not a filled trade, and neither number includes gas. Each trade is quoted once a day; prices move between runs.

This is a measurement, not a promise about any future quote and not financial advice.
