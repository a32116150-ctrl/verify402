Free · no wallet · before the signature

Check a token approval before you sign it.

An approval lets another address move your tokens. The free checker builds the classic unlimited approve(spender, max) from a token address and spender, asks the pre-signing gate to decode it, and shows the verdict in plain language. Nothing is signed, sent or paid.

Check an approval Three intents to refuse

01 · Why approvals matter

“Approve” is not the same as “send”, but it can be as powerful.

A wallet prompt usually shows a token and a spender. It rarely explains that an unlimited allowance lets that spender move the whole balance, now or later. If the spender is a new or unverified contract, the signature can outlive the trade you thought you were making.

The checker does not connect to your wallet. Paste the token contract, the spender from the signature prompt, and the network. It constructs the approval calldata locally in the browser and sends that intent to the free gate.

02 · What the gate reads

Calldata, contract, spender, simulation, block.

The transaction preflight decodes the call and reports the action, token, spender and amount. It checks the recipient, reads contract bytecode, applies the approval and spender policy, checks swap fields where present, runs an eth_call simulation when a from address is supplied, and reads a fresh block. Every response lists what ran and what did not.

With the sample, expect a BLOCK

The simple form builds an unlimited approval. With forbidUnlimitedApproval set, the gate returns BLOCK unlimited_approval. With an unknown spender you also get approval_unverified_spender; without a from address simulation is skipped and named as such. That is the gate telling the truth about its reach.

See it in dollars, with the fix

Add your public wallet address and the answer also says, in plain words, how much the spender could take today (for example “the spender could take your whole USDC balance: about $1.97 today, and anything you receive later”), whether the spender is a known protocol, an unknown contract, or a plain wallet (a common drainer pattern), and how to fix it: approve only the amount you need. Add a dollar risk limit and anything above it is a BLOCK.

03 · Reading the result

A PASS is narrow by design.

PASS means the listed checks passed at that block. It does not mean the token is a good investment, the spender has a good reputation, or the allowance is appropriate for your workflow. CAUTION means a decision is still required. BLOCK means a hard rule failed and an executor should refuse the signature.

If you want a separate pre-trade judgment on the token itself, the paid /safety check charges $0.02 and returns TRUST, CAUTION or AVOID from a quote-based sellability check. Transfer restrictions stay in notChecked.

04 · A safer habit

Prefer a bounded allowance you can explain.

Use the smallest allowance that supports the task, verify the spender's contract address from a trusted source, and review old approvals in your wallet or a reputable revocation tool. verify402 does not revoke approvals and does not hold keys. It gives your executor a checkable answer before the signature is made.

For the raw request shape and a curl example, see the preflight section of the docs. For an agent-safe enforcement pattern, see AI agent payment security.

Try it

One token. One spender. One honest answer.

The browser form needs no account, no API key and no wallet. The Base USDC sample returns a BLOCK so you can see the reason codes immediately.
