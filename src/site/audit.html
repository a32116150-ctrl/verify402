Six questions. "Don't know" is the finding.

Answer these about your own service. You do not need me to do this part — you need me if any answer is a guess.

QUESTION 01

Die between settle and record

The rail accepts the payment. The process dies before anything about that payment is durable. When the payer retries, what do they get — the response, a charge with nothing behind it, or a fresh 402?

QUESTION 02

Two copies, same payment

Verification is read-only. Send the same signed payment three times at once. How many times does your handler run? If the answer is "once, because we have a lock", where is the lock written down?

QUESTION 03

The answer while it is unknown

A retry arrives and you cannot yet tell whether the settlement landed. What status code goes back, and does that answer ever invite the caller to sign a second payment for the same purchase?

QUESTION 04

What deletes a paid response

A timer, a deploy, a cache eviction, a TTL. Which one removes the stored response for a payment that already settled — and how long after the caller last asked?

QUESTION 05

If it ends stuck

The outcome never resolves. Is the record kept, the key blocked, and a human told — or does it quietly become void so the dashboard stays green?

QUESTION 06

Who else can prove it

If I asked you to show me the crash test that kills the process at each of those points, could you run it in the next ten minutes?

Objections, answered

The questions you are about to ask.

"We use a facilitator — doesn't that handle this?"

The facilitator executes the settlement. It cannot know what your process did before the call, what it did after, or whether the record survived. The window between your settle call and your durable write is yours, not theirs. That is precisely where a payer gets charged twice or charged for nothing.

"We have tests. The suite is green."

Tests that do not kill the process mid-write cannot cross the window. A green suite tells you the happy path is intact; it says nothing about the four milliseconds between the rail accepting a payment and your store knowing about it. That is the only thing I test.

"Our volume is tiny. Why would anyone bother?"

A double charge is not a volume problem, it is a trust problem. The agent that got charged twice does not file a complaint — it stops calling, and so does its owner. One lost payer costs more than this review.

"Can you sign an NDA? Can we limit what you see?"

Yes, mutual NDA before any access. Scope can be one file, one module, or a redacted diagram. I do not need your users' data, your keys, or your production environment — and I will not ask for them.

"What if you find nothing?"

You get a written statement of what was checked, why it holds, and which assumptions would break it. That document is worth having: it is the thing you show a customer who asks whether your endpoint can lose their payment. Fees are not refunded, and the statement is the deliverable.
